Automatic predictive thermal control¶

A single-state predictive controller for the lecture-hall heat pump. It models how the room temperature responds to ON/OFF compressor decisions, then runs a re-observe-every-step loop that keeps each booked event inside the \(20.5\text{–}21.5\,^\circ\text{C}\) comfort band at minimal heating effort.

Introduction: the physical differential equation¶

The room is treated as a single lumped thermal mass. Its temperature changes at a rate set by the heat the compressor injects minus the heat lost to ambient:

\[C\,\frac{dT}{dt} \;=\; \underbrace{Q_\text{hp}}_{\text{compressor}} \;-\; \underbrace{Q_\text{loss}}_{\text{to ambient}} .\]

Dividing by the heat capacity \(C\) and absorbing constants into rate coefficients gives the working model — one state \(T\), two parameters:

\[\frac{dT}{dt} \;=\; G \cdot \text{duty} \;-\; L ,\]

where

  • \(T\) is room air temperature (°C), the average over the four devices,

  • \(\text{duty}\in[0,\,0.68]\) is the compressor duty fraction (0.68 is the empirical hardware ceiling — ON commands the cap, OFF commands 0),

  • \(G\) (°C/h) is the heating contribution per unit duty, and

  • \(L\) (°C/h) is a constant heat-loss rate.

The loss is taken constant rather than as an RC gap term \(-\,b\,(T-T_{out})\): across the dataset that gap term is a daytime-solar confound, not conduction, so a single net loss predicts better at the control horizon and does not blow up in open loop.

Generalized to AUTOMATIC mode. When the controller may both heat and cool, the gross term becomes directional and the direction is automatic, set by the room temperature relative to the \(T^{*}=21\,^\circ\text{C}\) target:

\[\begin{split}\frac{dT}{dt} \;=\; G_{\sigma}\cdot\text{duty} \;-\; L , \qquad \sigma \;=\; \begin{cases} \text{HEAT}, & T < T^{*} \\[2pt] \text{COOL}, & T > T^{*} \end{cases}\end{split}\]

with \(G_\text{HEAT}=+2.29\) and \(G_\text{COOL}=-1.74\;^\circ\text{C/h}\). Idling sets \(\text{duty}=0\), leaving the passive drift \(dT/dt=-L\). Discretised for the controller's step (forward Euler, \(\Delta t = \tfrac{1}{4}\,\text{h}\)):

\[T_{k+1} \;=\; T_k + \bigl(G_{\sigma_k}\cdot\text{duty}_k - L\bigr)\,\Delta t .\]

On the heating week the fit is \(dT/dt = 2.29\,\text{duty} - 0.50\), so the maximum heat-up at the duty cap is \(2.29\cdot0.68 - 0.50 = 1.06\;^\circ\text{C/h}\) — the slow capacity that is the binding constraint of the whole problem.

Generalized heuristic moving average¶

The two fixed coefficients \((G, L)\) are accurate but season-specific: they must be refit when conditions change, and they cannot see solar gain, occupancy or shifting outdoor temperature. The generalized controller replaces them with online rate estimates — an exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) of the realised temperature change in each mode. Because the estimates are built from observed \(\Delta T\), they absorb every disturbance implicitly and need no per-season refit; the fitted constants only seed them.

Driving equations¶

At each step the controller observes the realised instantaneous rate over the step just completed:

\[\rho_k \;=\; \frac{T_k - T_{k-1}}{\Delta t} \qquad [^\circ\text{C/h}] .\]

It maintains one rate per mode, \(r_m\) for \(m\in\{\text{HEAT},\text{COOL},\text{IDLE}\}\), and updates only the mode that was actually applied over the last step, with smoothing \(\alpha\in(0,1]\):

\[r_{\sigma_{k-1}} \;\leftarrow\; \alpha\,\rho_k \;+\; (1-\alpha)\,r_{\sigma_{k-1}} .\]

Each \(r_m\) is therefore a net rate: it already contains the loss and the disturbance, not just the compressor contribution. The estimates are seeded from the physical fit:

\[r_\text{HEAT}^{(0)} = G_\text{HEAT}\,u_\text{cap} - L = +1.06, \qquad r_\text{IDLE}^{(0)} = -L = -0.50, \qquad r_\text{COOL}^{(0)} = G_\text{COOL}\,u_\text{cap} - L .\]

Forecasting a candidate mode over a horizon \(H\) is then a single linear extrapolation from the current measured temperature (re-anchored every step):

\[\hat{T}_m(\tau) \;=\; T_k + r_m\,\tau , \qquad \tau\in[0, H] .\]

The act/idle decision compares the coast forecast (\(r_\text{IDLE}\)) against the band. To preheat, the lead time is back-solved from the acting rate in the auto-selected direction \(d_k\), with a 1.15× safety margin for residual model and weather error:

\[\text{lead}_k \;=\; \frac{T^{*} - T_k}{r_{d_k}}\times 1.15 .\]

Self-correction near equilibrium is automatic: as the room approaches steady state the realised \(\rho_k\) flattens, so the EWMA flattens the forecast too — removing the "cools forever" artefact that a fixed slope would produce.

The general loop¶

The controller is a receding-horizon loop run every \(\Delta t = 15\) minutes. Each step:

  1. Sense the room temperature \(T_k\) (average of the four devices).

  2. Update the EWMA for the mode just applied: \(r_{\sigma_{k-1}} \leftarrow \alpha\,\rho_k + (1-\alpha)\,r_{\sigma_{k-1}}\).

  3. Choose direction automatically: \(d_k = \text{HEAT}\) if \(T_k < T^{*}\), else \(\text{COOL}\).

  4. Forecast the coast and the acting trajectories over the horizon using \(\hat{T}_m(\tau) = T_k + r_m\,\tau\), and compute \(\text{lead}_k\).

  5. Decide ACT or IDLE — act in direction \(d_k\) at the duty cap if coasting would leave the band within the lead horizon (or an event is within \(\text{lead}_k\)); otherwise idle.

  6. Apply the chosen mode for one step, observe \(T_{k+1}\), set \(k\leftarrow k+1\), and repeat.

The fixed-coefficient model is the special case \(\alpha\to 0\): the rates stay frozen at their physical seeds and the loop reduces to the constant \((G, L)\) predictor.

The predict/ sandbox¶

predict/ is the research-and-modeling sandbox for the forecasting side of the controller. It collects several model families — linear regressors, gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost) and the TimesFM time-series foundation model — for forecasting room and device return temperatures and for profiling each device's power draw, fit separately per device and per operating mode. It is a live model rather than a one-off offline fit: it keeps learning as it goes, refining its temperature and power-draw estimates as new readings arrive.
